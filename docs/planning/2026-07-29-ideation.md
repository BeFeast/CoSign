# CoSign — Ideation Synthesis (2026-07-29)

> Synthesis of the CoSign thinking as of 2026-07-29. Sources: [`Ideation.md`](../product/Ideation.md) (thesis v1→v3), [`SPEC-v1.md`](../specs/SPEC-v1.md) (send-loop spec, draft), [`EXPLAINER.md`](../product/EXPLAINER.md), [`HANDOVER.md`](../HANDOVER.md), the original [`PRD.md`](../specs/PRD.md), and the Slack ideation session of 2026-07-28. If you are joining the project, this is the current state of thinking; the root docs are the detailed source of truth for build scope.

## 1. Thesis and the 99/1 insight

**One line:** a collab ledger for hybrid music creators (samples / beats / songs) that knows who made which layer, under which names, with PRO/credit info ready to send — and splits that only change when everyone approves.

The thesis has been through three versions. v1 was "provenance + co-sign approvals." v2 (after producer interviews, 2026-07-26) reframed the adoption problem: producers agree it saves time but will not adopt anything that feels like extra admin, so CoSign must *read work they already do* (filename conventions like `Velvet 95bpm Gm @antn42`). v3 (2026-07-28) is the current spine, triggered by a producer remark relayed in the ideation session:

> **~99% of songs never make money. Doing admin on all 1000 to protect the 1% is exactly why nobody does the paperwork.**

So the product posture is: **capture everything at ~zero cost, and only do real paperwork on the songs that show signs of life** (a placement, streams, an earnings signal while PRO registration is missing). Insurance that costs nothing until it pays off. This one insight unifies every feature idea collected so far: auto-capture makes the 99% free; a money trigger fires on the 1%.

## 2. Problem

From producer interviews plus industry input:

- Splits agreed in DMs and forgotten; a 50/50 can quietly become 70/30 with no record.
- Hybrid creators blur layers — "did he make the sample, or only a beat that uses the sample?" — and clearance months later becomes memory archaeology.
- The same work carries many names (project file → marketplace title → release title); different people remember different ones.
- Songs **earn while unregistered with a PRO** → directly lost income, and nobody notices.
- Contacts ("who do I call if this blows up") and contracts are scattered across chats and inboxes, disconnected from the earning track.
- The blocker to any fix: the paperwork cost lands at the worst moment (post-production slog), on songs that statistically will not earn.

## 3. Product model — four layers

1. **Front door: the send loop.** Export stems → pick a collaborator → send; they get an email and download. This is a chore producers already do, removed — not admin added. Crucially, the send captures the one thing no metadata ever holds: **who the collaborators are**, plus lineage — both parties are logged as a byproduct of the send. "Come for the sender, stay for the ledger."
2. **Capture (make the 99% free).** A ladder of optional on-ramps: filename parsing (`title bpm key @handles`, no install, always-confirm heuristics that learn per-user conventions), a watched export folder, and — deepest rung — the DAW bridge for exact BPM/key/track names. Identity is a **claimable producer tag** with aliases, graph-weighted verification (notable tags gated, obscure tags one-click); tag squatting cannot move money because splits still require co-sign.
3. **Trigger (monetize the 1%).** PRO-status tracker + earnings log (CSV import from PRO statements; live PRO APIs later) + alerts: *"this song is earning with incomplete PRO data — you are losing money right now."* On trigger, generate the split sheet, register, pull in contacts/contracts — paperwork just-in-time, not up front.
4. **Vault (source of truth per song).** One entry per work: roster and splits, title/AKA history, lineage, per-collaborator PRO/IPI, attached documents, song-linked contacts, revenue tags. Multi-party approval on any ownership change; catalog fields editable by the trusted roster.

## 4. Competitive picture (honest read)

The market exists — which validates the need — but the obvious wedge is partly taken:

- **SessionSplit** is the closest competitor: secure stem delivery + split-sheet signing + escrow + verified credits. Nearly the "send stems → log split" thesis.
- **Sound Credit** covers the vault side: file sharing + credits + splits + metadata export to PROs, subscription, takes no royalty cut.
- Also in the field: SplitChord (split sheets, e-sign), Highnote/Splice Studio/SyncMuse (stem delivery), Songtrust/Sentric (collection services taking a % — what CoSign deliberately is not).

**Not novel:** "send stems + split sheet + verified credits" (SessionSplit does it), and "0% royalty" (differentiates vs Songtrust only — Sound Credit and SessionSplit take no cut either; this corrects an earlier overstatement in our own pitch).

**The defensible wedge:** (a) zero-admin auto-capture — competitors are form-first or voice-dictation; nobody captures all 1000 songs for free from filenames/sends; (b) money-recovery alerts as an active watchdog — Sound Credit documents metadata, it does not nag about lost income; (c) the 99/1 posture itself — competitors are admin-first, "no paperwork until money shows up" is a philosophy, not a feature, and harder to copy. Positioning must move off "send + split" onto "auto-capture everything + tell me which songs are losing me money."

## 5. Where the POC stands (2026-07-29)

- **Client-side MVP is live** at cosign.befeast.com — full PRD core loop (works, rosters, even splits, proposal/approval flow, AKAs, lineage, credit packs, contacts, audit log) as a React SPA on **localStorage only, no backend**. Multi-user approval is demoed via an "acting as" identity switcher. A design "usability pass" shipped 2026-07-28 (plain language instead of "co-sign" jargon, needs-you lane, split bars).
- The data layer is deliberately swappable: all domain logic sits behind a single repo module (`src/data/repo.ts`), designed as the seam for a real backend. Consensus: **stop adding to the localStorage demo** — it is a dead end for the real product.
- **DAW bridge POC exists:** a bridge relays live session data (BPM, key, track/project names) from the DAW to a local server, cross-platform in principle. Verdict: a VST/extension install is itself friction, so the bridge is a **bonus deep layer, never the front door**. Track-name-first for stem naming; AI instrument recognition only as a fallback for unnamed channels, never built first.
- Name "CoSign" is a working name, **deferred until launch** (domains largely taken, weak SEO, differentiation no longer centered on the co-sign act).

## 6. v1 = the send loop (SPEC-v1, draft, not built)

The dumbest version that tests the wedge, per [`SPEC-v1.md`](../specs/SPEC-v1.md): magic-link auth → create a work → drag/drop stems (upload) → pick recipient by email/handle → default-even editable split → send. Recipient gets an email with a download link, sees who is on it and the proposed split, can accept it; both parties see the work in their catalog. Backend covers auth + database + storage + transactional email behind the repo swap point; the frontend reuses the existing app. Explicitly out of scope for v1: DAW bridge, AI naming, filename parsing, PRO tracker/alerts, PDFs/e-sign, escrow. Success bar: **5 real producers complete the loop and come back to send again.** Hard bits: upload UX, the email-claim flow, and per-user data isolation.

**Four open decisions — all UNDECIDED** (recommendations exist; see the discussion agenda):

1. **Auth:** magic link only (rec) vs also Google OAuth. — UNDECIDED
2. **Download gate:** download via signed link without an account (rec; gate only "accept the split") vs force sign-up. — UNDECIDED
3. **Split capture in v1:** capture it, default even, editable (rec — it is the differentiator) vs files-only, splits later. — UNDECIDED
4. **Limits/retention:** e.g. 500 MB–1 GB per send, files expire ~30 days, ledger record persists forever. — UNDECIDED

## 7. Fresh input — pushing the thinking further

*This section is new analysis, not a restatement of the root docs.*

- **Define what "5 producers send twice" actually measures.** A resend can mean "the upload was convenient" (a WeTransfer substitute) without validating the ledger at all. Instrument v1 to separate the two: did the recipient *view the split*, did anyone *accept*, did either party ever *reopen the work page* later? A crude proxy for ledger value: at least one work per pilot user that gets a second touch after the send. If sends repeat but splits are never looked at, we have validated file transfer, not CoSign — and the file-transfer market is a graveyard of free incumbents.
- **The recipient is the growth engine — design v1 around their first 60 seconds, not the sender's.** The email link is the entire viral loop. The recipient page should not look like a download portal; it should look like *their credit already exists* ("You're on 'Velvet' at 50% — claim it"). Endowment ("this is already yours") converts better than invitation. Recommendation for open decision #2 follows directly: never gate the download, always gate the claim.
- **Asymmetry risk in the two-sided claim flow.** The sender asserts the split unilaterally; the recipient's only options are accept or ignore. There is no "accept the credit, dispute the %" path in v1 — so an ignored split and a contested split look identical in the data. Even a one-click "I was on this, but the split is wrong" state would (a) keep the recipient in the funnel instead of bouncing, and (b) give us the single most interesting validation signal: do real collaborators actually disagree about splits? If they never do, the co-sign mechanism matters less than we think; if they often do, it is the product.
- **Storage cost is a positioning trap, not just an infra bill.** If CoSign hosts stems, every heavy sender is a cost center and the free tier bleeds. The 30-day expiry is right, but say it out loud in the product: *"files are the envelope, the record is the letter."* CoSign should never compete with Dropbox on retention — the permanent artifact is the ledger entry. This framing also cleanly answers "why do files expire?" without sounding cheap, and keeps a future "bring your own storage / relink expired files" option open.
- **Cold-start hedge: the send loop and filename batch-ingest solve different halves of it.** The send captures *new* collabs going forward; the filename drop capitalizes the *backlog* (1000 existing files → instant catalog). v1 correctly starts with the send, but the sequencing question worth pre-deciding is which one greets a new user who arrives via a claim email with nothing to send back. An empty catalog at that moment is where the loop dies; "drop your beats folder" is the natural second action for exactly that user.
- **The money-recovery hook needs data we do not control — start collecting evidence now.** CSV import from PRO statements assumes producers can export them and will bother. Before building layer 2, get 3–5 real PRO statements from the pilot circle and check parse-ability by hand. If real statements are messy or producers cannot produce them, the "earning but unregistered" alert — the sharpest retention hook — quietly dies, and that is better known before the backend exists.
- **Legal honesty as copy, early.** An accepted split in CoSign is evidence, not a contract. That is fine — timestamps + mutual acceptance are genuinely useful in disputes — but the copy must never promise enforceability, or the first dispute becomes a reputational incident. The PRD's "coordination tool, not a law firm" line should survive into v1 UI.
- **Watch SessionSplit deliberately, not anxiously.** They own "send + split" mindshare; our wedge only holds if auto-capture and the watchdog ship before they bolt on a filename parser. A quarterly competitive re-check (they ship fast in a small niche) is cheap insurance.
